F³ DRAFT ENGINE

How the draft-day recommendation engine thinks: the scoring formula, the opponent-monitoring model, the strategy overrides, and the 30-mock tuning run that shaped it. League: F³ — 16 teams, full PPR, draft Sunday Sep 6, 7:00 PM, 60 seconds per pick.

The one-line summary

Follow the Fantasy Footballers' tiers, tilted by John's scouting; never pay for a bust; hold cheap positions (QB, TE) until the moment the market is about to close them — and know that moment precisely, because the engine watches all 15 other rosters on every pick.

The scoring formula

At every turn the engine scores every available player and recommends the lowest score — think of it as an "effective draft rank."

score = UDK rank + tags − need×6 − VBD×0.15 + strategy overrides

UDK rank (backbone)1–200+The Ballers' full-PPR overall rank is the starting score. Every other force is a bounded adjustment, so the engine can only move a player a round or two — except in declared emergencies.

Lineup value (VBD)−0 to −1515% of projected points above this league's replacement level (QB18 / RB40 / WR44 / TE18). Teaches the engine that a 100-point TE edge beats a 30-point WR-bench edge.

Opponent monitoring — the survival model

For each candidate, the engine asks: will he still be there at my next pick? It walks every intervening pick and estimates that specific team's chance of taking him — a team already holding two RBs is far less likely to take a third (the original design insight), scaled by how overdue the player is versus ADP. Multiplying through the 15–30 picks between turns yields the survival percentage on the dashboard: 5% — now or never vs. 65% — safe to wait.

Survival is used twice: directly, to time non-urgent picks; and as the trigger for strategy escalations — the engine escalates on math, not vibes.

Strategy overrides

QB punt floor

Wait on QB while the tagged targets (Dak, Stafford, Purdy, Dart, Lawrence) survive. When the expected number surviving to the next pick drops below 1.5, the best target gets −45 and force-ranks. Fires mid-run, before the pool dies.

TE great-or-late

Take a tier-1 TE when the board says so (VBD handles it) — otherwise wait for the tagged late pool (Likely, Strange, Chig, Dulcich). Same survival trigger, −30, active from round 5.

Bench composition

From round 5: −8 per RB short of four; +12 per WR beyond four. Gated so it never distorts the early tier board (lesson learned when it briefly stole round 2 from tier-1 Trey McBride at 4% survival).

Hard rules

Never a 3rd QB/TE (+60) or 2nd K/D-ST (+200); no K/D-ST early (+120); in the final picks, missing K/D-ST slots get −100 — the lineup is always legal.

Lineup value — Bowers over WR #6 Brock Bowers projects ~100 points above the 18th TE (−15) while a sixth WR projects ~30 over waiver level (−4.5). Before the VBD term the engine hoarded WRs; after it, slot 16 took Bowers at the turn and slot 7 landed McBride — the "great" branch firing only when the price was right.

Wilson is UDK #20 with a round-4 ADP — the league's biggest full-PPR mispricing — and the survival model is what lets the engine harvest him a round late instead of reaching.
